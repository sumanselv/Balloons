About Pop Balloons

A calm, free interactive game for a quiet moment: colorful balloons drift up through a sky that slowly changes from sunrise to moonlit night.

How to play

There is nothing to win and no timer. Click or tap a balloon to pop it, or press the first letter of the element symbol written on it (for Na, press N), and hear a soft piano note. You can also pop the soap bubbles that float up from the bottom of the screen. Hover over a balloon with your mouse and it will shine. Use the speaker button in the top-left corner to turn the sound on or off.

Atoms and ions

Every balloon is a tiny piece of chemistry. Each one is labeled with the chemical symbol of an element, such as H for hydrogen, Na for sodium or Cl for chlorine. The small number at the top-left of the symbol is the element's atomic number, which is how many protons its atom has. The two glowing dots circling each balloon stand for electrons orbiting the nucleus.

If a symbol has a small charge beside it, like Na+ or O2−, the balloon is an ion. An ion is an atom that has lost or gained electrons. Losing electrons gives a positive charge, as with sodium (Na+) and calcium (Ca2+). Gaining electrons gives a negative charge, as with chloride (Cl−) and oxide (O2−). Balloons with no charge, like neon (Ne), are neutral atoms. When you pop a balloon, you see its name and symbol, and a few electrons fly out of positive ions or rush into negative ions.

Ionic bonds

Opposite charges attract, and the balloons follow the same rule. When a positive ion and a negative ion with matching charges float near each other, they drift together. If they touch, they bond and merge into one glowing balloon showing the compound, for example Na+ and Cl− becoming NaCl (sodium chloride, or table salt), or Mg2+ and O2− becoming MgO (magnesium oxide). Two ions with the same charge repel each other and push apart.

What you will see

The page starts with just balloons under a rising sun. As you stay, more of the world appears, one thing at a time:

  • Bubbles, clouds and blowing leaves
  • Birds, parrots, peacocks, butterflies, dragonflies, bees and ladybirds
  • Kites, parachutes, airplanes pulling banners and helicopters
  • Rain followed by a rainbow
  • Fireworks that spell out kind words in the sky, and rockets
  • At night: the moon, twinkling stars, shooting stars, fireflies and satellites

Kind words

Every so often a crumpled scrap of paper blows across the screen, smooths out and sticks to the page for a few seconds so you can read the short, positive quote written on it. Banners on the airplanes and helicopters carry encouraging messages too. The idea is to leave you feeling a little lighter than when you arrived.

Why it exists

Pop Balloons was made as a gentle, screen-friendly way to take a break. It runs in your web browser on phones, tablets and computers, and it needs no account and no download.
